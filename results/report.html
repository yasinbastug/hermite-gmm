Hermite-GMM Benchmark

A Gaussian mixture whose components carry exactly-normalized squared-Hermite shape corrections (degrees 1–2 excluded, unit-norm gauge fixed), fit by monotone block-coordinate generalized EM — benchmarked against plain GMM and a Student's-t mixture on ten standard datasets.

  • Headline result on 5 of 7 eligible datasets (wine13, diabetes, thyroid, AIS, wine27): the Hermite-GMM density fitted at the true G beats the plain GMM's BIC-selected model — which used more components — on 5-fold held-out log-likelihood. Fewer, more flexible components beating more, rigid ones.
  • AIS is the cleanest win. CV selects exactly the true G = 2 with degree-4 corrections: ARI 0.884 vs 0.717 for the BIC-selected 3-component GMM, and held-out log-likelihood −4.75 vs −5.85 per sample. On the paper's 2-variable AIS task the Hermite fit scores 180/202 correct — matching the unrestricted skew-t literature number (183) and beating restricted skew-t (157) and symmetric-t (165).
  • Correctness checks all pass. The reduction to sklearn's GaussianMixture is exact to 2·10⁻¹⁵; the penalized EM objective is monotone non-decreasing on every dataset; the 1-D synthetic test recovers a pure degree-3 (skew) correction with the right sign.
  • Gauge fixing matters as much as Section 33 claims. On the unit sphere the inner problem is essentially perfectly conditioned (cond ≈ 1.6–4.8) and EM converges in 4–6 outer iterations; the naive raw-b parameterization is 470–1400× worse conditioned, takes 83–105 iterations, and lets ‖b‖ collapse to 0.08–0.31 so the ridge penalty shrinks meaningless scale instead of shape.
  • BIC never favors the Hermite model — it charges the full raw coefficient count (e.g. +455 per component for degree 3 in 13 dimensions), which the design doc itself flags as over-penalizing regularized coefficients. All headline wins are on held-out likelihood, the criterion the doc recommends.

Which non-Gaussianity did it find?

Degree-block energies Σ|α|=d b² are the whitening-invariant diagnostic. AIS — the dataset where the correction helps most — puts roughly a third of each component's energy into degrees 3 + 4, split between skew and tail weight, which matches its role as the skew-t literature's showcase dataset:

AIS, component 0 (G = 2, m = 4, λ = 10)

deg 0 (Gaussian)0.671
deg 3 (skew)0.154
deg 4 (kurtosis)0.175

Diabetes, component 2 (G = 3, m = 3, λ = 0.1)

deg 0 (Gaussian)0.829
deg 3 (skew)0.171

Degrees 1–2 are structurally zero (excluded from the basis per Section 26). On most datasets the correction is modest and concentrated at degree 3 — genuine skew, not spread-out high-degree noise. The exception is wine27, where 97% of the energy sits in 3,654 degree-3 terms — a warning sign of overfitting in p = 27 with n = 178, consistent with CV choosing G = 1 there.

The naive runs' higher raw train LL (≈ +0.005–0.08) is the predicted artifact: shrinking ‖b‖ weakens the effective ridge, so λ stops doing its job — exactly the "two unrelated jobs" failure Section 33 describes.

Caveats

The t-mix baseline is not a skew-t. No R is available on this machine and EMMIXskew/EMMIXuskew are archived on CRAN, so the baseline is the pure-Python studenttmixture — a symmetric Student's-t mixture that captures heavy tails but not skewness. The AIS literature table above supplies the true skew-t reference numbers for at least that dataset. Other caveats: BIC for the Hermite model charges the raw coefficient count and therefore over-penalizes (Section 36); wine27's m = 4 cell was skipped (basis larger than 10,000); crabs at G = 4 is unsolved by every model tested.
